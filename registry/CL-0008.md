Question: Who gave themselves away?
Clues: 1) Inspector said only 'Lord Ashby is dead' — no cause, no place | 2) Gardener's scratched hands (pruning roses) | 3) Secretary: 'Who would push him down those stairs?'
False lead: The gardener's scratched hands.
Solution: The secretary
Explanation: The inspector only said 'Lord Ashby is dead' — no cause, no place. The secretary mentioned the stairs and a push, details only the killer could know. The gardener's scratches come from pruning roses (false lead); the cook's questions are what an innocent person asks.
Mechanism (do not reuse): Guilty knowledge: a suspect reveals an unreleased detail (cause and place of death).

## Narration script

The inspector said only four words. The killer answered a question nobody asked.
Morning at Blackwood Manor. Inspector Hale gathers the staff and says just this: Lord Ashby is dead. Nothing about how. Nothing about where.
The gardener, his hands covered in fresh scratches, goes pale and says nothing.
The cook gasps: Dead? When? I served him dinner at eight.
The secretary sighs: Poor man. Who would push him down those stairs?
Who gave themselves away? You have five seconds.
The secretary. Nobody had said how Ashby died, or where.
She knew about the stairs and the push, because she was there. The gardener's scratches? He'd been pruning roses. Only the killer knows what the police haven't said.
