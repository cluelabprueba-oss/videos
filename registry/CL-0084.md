Mechanism (do not reuse): Reflex to one's own name: shouting a fugitive's real name makes only the fugitive turn, even briefly; packed suitcase and nerves are false leads.
Script (EN): A fugitive can change his name. But not what his brain does when he hears it. A wanted man, Edmund Crane, hides in a boarding house under a false name. Three new lodgers. At dinner, the detective calls out from the back of the room: Edmund! Kit has a packed suitcase by the door. When the name rings out, he keeps stirring his tea. Leon says he is a retired teacher. When the name rings out, his head turns for half a second. Mara is nervous, and stares at the exit. When the name rings out, she keeps staring. Who is Edmund Crane? You have five seconds. Five. Four. Three. Two. One. It was Leon. We react to our own name before we can think, even a man who has hidden it for years. Kit's suitcase and Mara's nerves were false leads. Only Leon turned.
